ABSTRACT
Multimodal survival analysis aims to integrate pathological images, genomics, and other modalities for accurate patient prognosis prediction. Recent concatenation- and attention-based methods have shown promising potential in pathology-omics fusion, yet three key challenges remain.
First, pathological and genomic reports are often overlooked; they contain higher-level semantics such as histological morphology, molecular abnormalities, and prognostic clues, providing additional supplementary information for pathological images and genomic features.
Second, whole-slide images contain a large number of redundant patch tokens. Directly feeding them into models introduces substantial computational cost and may interfere with the learning of prognostically relevant features. Third, pathological images, genomics, and textual semantics exhibit strong heterogeneity and high-dimensional feature spaces, making it difficult to learn stable and discriminative patient-level representations.
To address these challenges, we propose CRMSurv: Learning \underline{C}ompact and \underline{R}isk-discriminated \underline{M}ultimodal Representations for Cancer \underline{Surv}ival Analysis. Specifically, we first construct semantic representations from pathology and genomic reports, and design a dual-branch Q-Former that uses learnable query tokens as information bottlenecks to extract compact survival-relevant representations from numerical features and textual semantics. We then introduce a pathology feature compression strategy without manually defined priors, which selects informative features and compresses redundant ones based on approximation error. Finally, we propose patient-level risk-discriminated learning based on a dynamic risk prototype bank to enhance risk-aware feature discrimination and generalization. Extensive experimental results on five cancer datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our model. The code will be made publicly available.
