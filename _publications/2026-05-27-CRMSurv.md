ABSTRACT
Multimodal survival analysis improves prognosis prediction by integrating heterogeneous data, including pathology, genomics, and textual reports. While most existing frameworks focus on cross-modal fusion, effective learning also depends on the quality of modality representations prior to interaction. Refinement of these representations still faces two main challenges. First, the severe computational burdens and high-frequency redundancies inherent in massive whole slide image (WSI) patches make compression essential;
however, existing methods struggle to balance representation compactness with the preservation of informative local evidence. Second, the high dimensionality of multimodal data paired with limited cohort scales makes models prone to overfitting frequent yet prognosis-irrelevant noise. To address these issues, we propose CRMSurv, a novel framework that learns \underline{C}ompact and \underline{R}isk-discriminated \underline{M}ultimodal representations for cancer \underline{Surv}ival analysis. Specifically, we develop Reconstructability-aware Patch (RP) Compression, combining compact prototypes and high-reconstruction-error patches to optimally balance primary morphology and complementary local details with fewer tokens. Furthermore, we introduce patient-level Risk-discriminated Learning (RL) with a dynamic risk prototype bank to explicitly supervise representation learning, mitigating overfitting to irrelevant noise by anchoring representations to stable prognostic patterns. Finally, we adopt multi-branch Q-Formers and cross-attention to align these refined representations for multimodal fusion. On six cancer datasets, CRMSurv yields an average 1\% improvement over the strongest baseline while retaining just 25\% of WSI patches.
