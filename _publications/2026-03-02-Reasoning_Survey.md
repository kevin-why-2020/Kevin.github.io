ABSTRACT
Multimodal medical reasoning, a cornerstone of clinical decision-making, is essential for accurate diagnosis, treatment planning, and patient management. With the rapid progress of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal foundation models, enabling machines to reason over heterogeneous medical data has become a central challenge in medical artificial intelligence. This survey presents a comprehensive review of multimodal medical reasoning in the era of multimodal large language models. Specifically, we categorize existing methods into three stages: \emph{multimodal data understanding}, which focuses on reasoning-oriented data curation and representation learning; \emph{multimodal knowledge utilization}, which studies how external medical knowledge is constructed, retrieved, and integrated during inference; and \emph{multimodal agentic analysis}, where reasoning actively guides evidence exploration, hypothesis refinement, and action selection in dynamic clinical environments. These stages delineate a trajectory of increasing decision-making autonomy, offering a principled roadmap for advancing multimodal medical reasoning from model-centric approaches toward agentic and system-level clinical intelligence. To support reproducibility and ongoing research, we maintain an up-to-date repository of papers and resources at \url{https://github.com/ZJUMAI/Multimodal-Medical-Reasoning}.
